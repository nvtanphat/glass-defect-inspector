What actually happened

reading across all three runs
01

Architecture tuning mattered more than architecture choice

RT‑DETR's first attempt scored 0.147 mAP50‑95 — worse than either CNN detector by a wide margin. The cause wasn't the transformer itself: deterministic=True is documented as unsafe for RT‑DETR on CUDA (its deformable attention has no deterministic backward pass), and the YOLO26 mosaic-heavy augmentation recipe was carried over unchanged, which works against DETR's Hungarian matching. Fixing both and resuming from checkpoint is the run currently in progress.

02

YOLO11m trades recall for precision

YOLO11m's precision (0.802) beats YOLO26m's (0.747) by five points, but recall drops nine points (0.586 vs. 0.679). For a defect inspection line, a missed flaw is usually costlier than a false alarm — which keeps YOLO26m ahead on the metric that matters operationally, not just the aggregate mAP.

03

The bottleneck is data, not model capacity

Every model tested puts DiVat and LoiTray at the bottom of its per-class ranking — the two rarest classes in the training set, at 837 and 223 images respectively. Larger or different architectures won't fix a class the model has seen a few hundred times; the next real gain is targeted data collection or class-aware augmentation for those two labels.

04

Fixed batch size beat AutoBatch on this GPU

Ultralytics' AutoBatch OOM'd mid-probe at a 70% memory target on the T4, then silently fell back to batch=2 — four times slower per epoch than necessary. A fixed batch=8, set directly, trained reliably for all three models.
